# System Architecture

**LORI Jury-Based Judgment System v2.0 - Multi-AI Agent Architecture**

---

## Public Architecture Boundary

The public architecture defines the visible governance structure of the system:

1. Case intake
2. AI role assignment
3. Independent role-based analysis
4. Cross-role deliberation
5. Public voting record
6. Human sovereignty review
7. Independent audit confirmation
8. Final verdict publication
9. Case archive

This file describes governance flow only. It does not disclose private routing, prompt isolation, audit memory design, scoring calibration, or anti-contamination mechanisms.

---

## Jury Composition

| Layer | Role | Public Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI Jury | Fact Finder | Establishes relevant facts and evidence boundaries |
| AI Jury | Legal Analyst | Identifies applicable legal, regulatory, or rule-based considerations |
| AI Jury | Moral Evaluator | Evaluates ethical implications and moral tradeoffs |
| AI Jury | Cultural Interpreter | Reviews cultural, social, and contextual sensitivity |
| AI Jury | Emotional Moderator | Monitors emotional distortion, manipulation risk, and tone imbalance |
| Human Layer | Main Sovereignty Judge | Holds final human judgment authority |
| Human Layer | Independent Auditor | Reviews process integrity and traceability |

---

## Governance Layers

### 1. Public Concept Layer

Defines the reason the system exists, the public role structure, and the human-sovereignty boundary.

### 2. Case Documentation Layer

Provides structured case records, role outputs, vote summaries, dissent notes, and verdict templates.

### 3. Audit and Traceability Layer

Requires every decision to leave a reviewable record of role participation, vote logic, human review, and final judgment.

### 4. Human Sovereignty Layer

Prevents AI consensus from becoming automatic authority. AI outputs remain advisory until reviewed and validated by the human sovereignty layer.
