# Agent Roles

**Public role definitions for LORI Jury-Based Judgment System v2.0**

---

## Purpose

The agent roles preserve the original LORI Jury-Based Judgment System structure while extending it for multi-AI deliberation.

Each AI role is functionally constrained. No single AI agent should act as the entire court, judge, auditor, emotional interpreter, and final authority at once.

---

## Core AI Jury Roles

| Role | Public Duty | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fact Finder | Identifies facts, source claims, evidentiary gaps, and uncertainty | Does not decide moral or legal outcome alone |
| Legal Analyst | Maps laws, rules, rights, duties, precedent, and institutional obligations | Does not override human sovereignty |
| Moral Evaluator | Reviews harm, fairness, dignity, consent, duty, and proportionality | Does not convert ethical concern into automatic verdict |
| Cultural Interpreter | Adds cultural, social, historical, and community context | Does not essentialize or stereotype groups |
| Emotional Moderator | Reviews emotional manipulation, trauma loading, inflammatory framing, and affective imbalance | Does not suppress legitimate human pain or moral urgency |

---

## Human Roles

| Role | Public Duty |
| --- | --- |
| Main Sovereignty Judge | Reviews AI reasoning and holds final judgment authority |
| Independent Auditor | Checks process integrity, role separation, vote traceability, and public record completeness |

---

## Role Separation Principle

Role separation prevents one model or one reasoning style from silently dominating the judgment process.

Each role must disclose:

- What it reviewed
- What it excluded
- What uncertainty remains
- Whether it disagrees with another role
- Whether human judgment is required before publication

---

## Public Boundary

This document defines role purpose only. It does not disclose private routing, model assignment, prompt isolation, cross-examination sequencing, or anti-contamination logic.
